# CS 255 Portfolio – DriverPass System Analysis and Design

## DriverPass Project

The DriverPass project involved designing a system for a client that wanted to improve the way students prepare for their driving tests. DriverPass wanted a web-based system where customers could create accounts, select training packages, take online practice tests, schedule driving lessons, and track their progress. The system also needed to support employees such as secretaries, driving instructors, the owner, and the IT administrator. Throughout the project, I analyzed the client's requirements and translated them into business requirements and system designs.

## What I Did Well

One part of the project that I did particularly well was identifying the different users of the DriverPass system and connecting their needs to specific system functions. Instead of looking at the system only from the customer's perspective, I considered what instructors, office staff, management, and IT personnel would need. I also think the UML diagrams helped me organize the system visually and understand how different parts of the system interact.

## What I Would Improve

If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve some of my UML diagrams. Creating the diagrams helped me realize how important it is to show enough detail without making the design difficult to understand. I would review the relationships, labels, and flow of each diagram and look for opportunities to make them clearer and more consistent. I would also make sure that each diagram connects directly to the requirements identified earlier in the project.

## Interpreting User Needs

I interpreted the user's needs by reviewing the DriverPass interview and identifying what each stakeholder expected from the system. For example, customers needed to schedule driving lessons and take practice tests, while instructors needed access to their schedules and the ability to provide information about lessons. Management needed reporting capabilities, and IT needed administrative access to maintain user accounts and the system.

Considering user needs is important because a system can function correctly from a technical perspective and still fail if it does not solve the problems of the people using it. Understanding the users first helps ensure that the final system is useful, accessible, and aligned with the client's business goals.

## My Approach to Software Design

My approach to designing software is to first understand the problem and gather requirements before deciding how the system should be built. I would identify the users, their goals, the major system functions, and any technical or security requirements. I would then break the system into smaller components and use models such as use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams to understand it from different perspectives.

In future projects, I would continue using an iterative approach. Instead of expecting the first design to be perfect, I would create an initial design, review it against the requirements, collect feedback, and improve it. I would also maintain clear documentation throughout the project so that developers, testers, clients, and other team members can understand the reasoning behind the design.
